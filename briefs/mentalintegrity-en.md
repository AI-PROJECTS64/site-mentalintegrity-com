## 1. Four primary references with distinct scopes

## 1 - European Union: Charter, Article 3

Article 3 recognizes respect for physical and mental integrity. In medicine and biology, it refers in particular to free and informed consent. This establishes the legal use of the expression; it gives no special status to a domain name.

## 2 - Chile: Law 21.383, 2021

This amendment to Article 19(1) of the Constitution links scientific and technological development to respect for life and physical and psychological integrity. It provides for particular protection of brain activity and information derived from it. Its scope is national; it is not a worldwide neurotechnology regime.

## 3 - UNESCO: recommendation adopted 11 November 2025

The Recommendation on the Ethics of Neurotechnology covers medical and non-medical uses, neural data and certain inferences about mental states. It proposes principles and implementation measures for Member States. It is a recommendation, not a certification or evidence that a product complies with applicable requirements.

## 4 - Council of Europe: report published 1 April 2026

Dated March 2026, the rapporteurs' report records the workshop of 18 November 2025 on human rights and neurotechnologies. It considers protection under existing rights and questions that remain open. The authors' views do not necessarily reflect the Council of Europe's official policy.

## Reading these references

These documents differ in authorship, territorial scope and effect. They establish neither demand to acquire the domains, a need for one address, nor financial value. Placing them together does not create a new obligation.
